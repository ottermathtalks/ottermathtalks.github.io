There is a pretty complex-geometric argument, which I call "the two-circle trick", that has come up recurrently in the classification programs of operator algebras, which allows one to replace isomorphisms by graded isomorphisms and thereby simplifies the classification. In recent work with Marcel Scherer, Eli Shamovich and Mansi Suryawanshi, this handy tool seemed to have reached its limit. I will describe what the obstacle was and how it was overcome by an unexpected application of Lie groups, giving rise to a new version of the two-circle trick.

My goal in this talk is to describe this argument, starting with the operator algebraic motivation, and then stripping away the operator algebraic paraphernalia and focusing on the complex geometric essence. We shall look at one application of the trick, which is a proof that whenever two homogeneous varieties are biholomorphic, there is a biholomorphism that preserves the origin. Along the way, I hope to get across a story of how the research was done, what AI had to do with it, and how we traveled from operator algebras to several complex variables and then to Lie groups and back.
